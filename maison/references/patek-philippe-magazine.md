# Study note: a watch house's printed magazine (Patek Philippe Magazine)

Fetched 2026-11-20 from the house's page about its magazine.

## Facts
- Launched 1996; editorial direction by the house's head, later passed to the next generation.
- **Twice a year, eight languages**, close to 300,000 copies to owners in 180+ countries.
- Not sold on newsstands: it is a **collectible gift to owners**.

## Subjects
Culture broadly: crafts (the example given is a coat tradition from Edo-period Japan),
artisanship, science and invention (historical exhibitions, observatory chronometry contests),
and the house's own archive.

## How the product appears
Watches appear **as subjects of a story** — a mechanism explained, a historical record told —
never as a standalone advertisement on an editorial page. Covers often show an object that is not
a watch (a hat by a milliner, a collector's sculptural chairs).

## Voice and image
Curatorial and explanatory; technical precision balanced with history. Commissioned editorial
photography, archive documents, behind-the-scenes material.

## Principles taken
Culture first, product inside stories (1); the cover as one singular, non-product object (2);
past informs present via the archive (4); slow biannual cadence, many languages, the magazine as a
gift (8); a precise, craft-literate voice (10).
